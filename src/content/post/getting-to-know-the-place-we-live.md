title: Getting to Know the Place We Live
---

I went to the Skagit Valley Museum because I wanted to feel closer to the fields, the sky, and the work of farming. I’ve been meaning to visit for a long time. Our project is also about becoming more aware of Skagit County, so visiting a museum devoted to the history of this place felt like a natural connection.

I especially loved the photographs of the farm fields. They showed a special quality of light and angles I don’t usually get to see. I enjoyed reading the little stories about the farms and getting a glimpse of their humanity. There was something beautiful about seeing the land and the human effort so closely connected.
The exhibit brought together photographs, maps, and stories about the dike, the waterworks, settlement, and the agricultural history of the valley. I recognized places I’d visited, including towns like Avon, Allen, and Hickman that are no longer towns in the same way. Seeing them together on a map and understanding their connections gave me a more layered sense of the place I live. It wasn’t just learning local history. I felt it in my body.

There was also an exhibit of portraits of Skagit Valley farmworkers. Looking at those photographs, I found myself imagining other people out in the fields, looking at the same sunlight, feeling the same frost, and doing the work of bringing food in. I felt less alone in my relationship with the land. I became more aware of all the people whose effort goes into feeding the people of this valley.

In everyday life, when I turn to the internet and social media, I’m often confronted with intense images of tragedy and events happening far away, where I have little ability to make an impact. There was something so wholesome about spending time with these photographs, the land, the maps, and the stories of the food we eat every day. The exhibit helped me feel grounded in this place.

I left wanting to meet more of the people who grow our food. I want to know the farmers close to me, support them, work alongside them, and develop a closer relationship with both the land and the people who care for it.

I hope we can all move in small steps toward knowing the place we live, and toward knowing each other.
