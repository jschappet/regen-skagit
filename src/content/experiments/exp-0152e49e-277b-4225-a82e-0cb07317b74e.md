## The Curiosity

What if we designed our homes around the warmth of the sun
instead of relying primarily on machines to keep us comfortable?

We've been imagining a village of small, earthen homes arranged
in a semicircle around a shared courtyard.

The homes face south, welcoming the low winter sun through
large windows. Thick walls and floors absorb warmth during
the day and release it as temperatures fall.

But before building a village, what could we learn from
building one little shed?

## What We Want to Try

Build a small, sun-oriented structure using simple materials
and passive-solar principles.

We want to explore how the position of the sun changes
throughout the seasons, how different materials store warmth,
and what makes a space feel comfortable.

The experiment could begin with a simple shed, greenhouse,
or tiny room.

We might measure temperatures inside and outside, compare
different thermal materials, and spend time experiencing
the space ourselves.

## Our Questions

- How much warmth can we capture from the winter sun?
- What materials store and release heat effectively?
- How does the orientation of the building change its performance?
- Can we create a comfortable space with very little purchased energy?
- What would we do differently if we built five homes instead of one shed?

## What Success Might Look Like

We build something small enough to learn from.

We discover what works, what doesn't, and what surprises us.

We share our observations so others can build on what we've learned.

Maybe the shed becomes a greenhouse, a workshop, or the
beginning of something larger.

Maybe we discover that our assumptions were wrong.

Either way, we've learned something together.
