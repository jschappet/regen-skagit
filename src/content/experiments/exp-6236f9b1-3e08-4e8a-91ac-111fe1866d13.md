## The Curiosity

What if belonging didn't require participation?

We've noticed something interesting about gathering in groups.

Sometimes people want conversation, connection, and activity.
Other times they want to sit quietly, observe, or simply
be near others without interacting.

But declining an invitation can create its own discomfort.

People may wonder what's wrong. They might ask questions,
offer encouragement, or try to include someone who would
rather be left alone.

What if we created a shared language that made it easier
to communicate how available we feel?

## What We Want to Try

Experiment with a simple, voluntary way for people to
communicate their openness to interaction.

Perhaps we use colored hats, ribbons, cards, or another
visible signal.

For example:

- **Green:** I'd enjoy conversation or an invitation.
- **Yellow:** I'm happy being here, but please check before engaging.
- **Gray:** I'm enjoying my own space right now.

These aren't labels for someone's emotions or personality.

They're invitations to respect what someone wants
in the present moment.

Anyone can change their signal whenever they wish,
and nobody needs to explain why.

We could try this during a meal, workshop, or community gathering.

Participation in the experiment would be optional.

## Our Questions

- Does having a visible signal make it easier to relax in a group?
- Do people feel more comfortable declining invitations?
- Does knowing someone's preferences make approaching them easier?
- What happens when someone changes their signal?
- Can we honor someone's desire for solitude without making them feel excluded?
- Does the system create freedom, or another expectation?

## What Success Might Look Like

Someone can sit quietly without being asked what's wrong.

Someone who wants connection can communicate that easily.

People feel free to participate, observe, leave, or return.

And perhaps we discover that we don't need colored hats at all.

Maybe the experiment helps us develop a culture where asking
and respecting boundaries becomes natural.

## An Invitation

Have you ever wanted to be around people without having to interact?

Have you ever wanted to join something but weren't sure
whether you'd be welcome?

**Come experiment with belonging that leaves room for
everyone to be themselves.**
