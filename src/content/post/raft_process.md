# The RAFT experiment journey

Everyone begins with their own interest and develops their own experiment. A coworking partner provides two opportunities to present, listen, ask questions, and celebrate. Each person works on their own project throughout.

## 1. Find an interest

What has your attention? What are you curious about, drawn toward, or itching to try? Start with your own interest and why it matters to you.

## 2. Private brain dump

Get your thoughts out of your head. Capture questions, possibilities, people, places, materials, wild ideas, and things that might get in the way.

Use this space to find a small experiment you might want to explore. Your notes stay private.

## 3. Make an invitation

Decide what kind of presentation partner you’re looking for. If your interest is gardening and your emerging focus is compost, you might want someone who is:

- Also exploring compost.
- Interested in gardening more broadly.
- Working through RAFT on any topic.

Choose either—or both—of these ways to reach someone:

- **Ask RAFT participants:** post an open invitation on the open experiments board. Other participants can browse the board and respond. People can opt in to notifications when someone posts a new invitation.
- **Invite a friend:** send an email inviting someone you know to participate. They start their own journey with their own personal interest.

The invitation explains the commitment: two short meetings, one after each week, where each person presents their own work and participates in Q&A.

## 4. Confirm your partner or choose to start alone

When someone responds to your board post or accepts your email invitation, agree together to become presentation partners. Each person chooses and owns their own experiment.

You can also explicitly choose to continue alone.

## 5. Set the two meeting dates

Agree on dates for:

- **The week-one meeting:** present your research, preparations, and intended action, then ask each other questions.
- **The week-two meeting:** share what happened, reflect on the experience, and celebrate.

If you’re working alone, set dates for your own two reviews.

## 6. Start the clock

Confirm your start date based on the meeting schedule. This is when the two-week experiment begins.

Finding an interest, posting an invitation, or confirming a partner does not automatically start the clock.

## 7. Explore, prepare, and present

Use week one to make your experiment possible. Gather data, research questions, collect materials, make calls, talk to people, and work out what you need.

At the first meeting, each person presents:

- What they explored and discovered.
- The experiment or action they intend to try in week two.
- Questions or uncertainties they’re still holding.

Follow each presentation with a short Q&A. Use what you learn to refine your own next step.

## 8. Move toward action and do it

Use week two to carry out your experiment. Keep the action manageable and allow yourself to adjust as you learn.

Privately record what happens, including surprises, obstacles, changes of direction, and how the experience feels. If you can’t carry out the action, that experience is still worth reflecting on.

## 9. Celebrate, meet, and review

Meet with your partner for the second presentation and Q&A. Each person shares their own experience and findings.

Look back at what you tried, what happened, what changed, and what you’re taking away. Celebrate the attention and effort you gave it, whatever the outcome.

If you’re working alone, take this time for your own review and celebration.

## 10. Tell your story

Create a public story using selected information from the beginning of your journey and five reflection questions that bring out the feelings within the action.

The story connects your original curiosity with what you experienced, how it felt, and what it means to you now. You choose what to share from your private workspace.

The five story questions still need to be defined.

## 11. Review and submit

Preview the story, author name, and public map location. Check that you’re comfortable sharing everything shown.

Submit the story for publishing review. Your private brain dump and working notes stay private. After review and publication, your story becomes part of the experiment map.
